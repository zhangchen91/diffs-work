## Why it is worth building

The daily life of legal and investment research: a contract goes through seven revisions in Word, and
in the end someone has to answer "what actually changed in revision seven versus revision three".
Today the routine is to close the document, open a browser, drag two files in — not many steps, but it
interrupts what you were doing, so many people settle for a quick glance with Word's "Compare
Documents", which is precisely the least reliable way to read it.

## How we plan to do it

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | In the task pane, choose "compare with a version I saved" or "compare with another document" |
| 2 | Use Office.js to obtain the OOXML packages of both documents (the zips behind `.docx` / `.xlsx`) |
| 3 | Hand them to the local site (`127.0.0.1:5279`) or to this site's wasm kernel for comparison |
| 4 | The result appears in the task pane as **a list plus navigation**: click an entry to jump to a paragraph / a cell |

## Would it introduce new leak risk

This is the point we want to state first: **documents will not be sent to the cloud**.

- going through the local site, the document stays on your machine, and its path does not pass through
  any third party;
- going through the in-page kernel, the file stays in browser memory, which the network panel can
  prove;
- the add-in itself needs no "upload document content" permission.

The minimum permissions the add-in requests are `ReadDocument` (Word) / `ReadWriteDocument` (Excel,
only when navigation is needed).

## The alternative workflow you can use today

1. In Word, "Save As" the two versions (or use the historical versions in git / your cloud drive);
2. open diffs.work and drag them in;
3. carry the conclusions back into the document.

Until the add-in ships, these three steps are already a complete, reviewable workflow — the add-in
saves **switching cost**, not capability.
